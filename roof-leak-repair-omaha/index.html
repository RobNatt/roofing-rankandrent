Roof Leak Repair in Omaha, NE

A brown ring on the ceiling or a drip during rain usually means water is getting past the shingles somewhere. Here is how leaks happen on Omaha roofs, how a roofer finds them, and how to get one out to your home.

Signs your roof is leaking

  • Brown or yellow rings on a ceiling, often near an outside wall or under a chimney.
  • Paint peeling or bubbling on upper-floor ceilings or walls.
  • Drips or the sound of water in the attic or walls during heavy rain.
  • Wet or flattened insulation, dark stains on the roof boards, or rusty nail tips in the attic.
  • A musty smell upstairs that gets worse after rain.

If water is coming in right now, see emergency roof repair for what to do in the first hour.

Where Omaha roofs usually leak

Shingles in the open field of the roof are rarely the first thing to fail. Leaks usually start where the roof is interrupted or where two surfaces meet:

  • Chimney flashing. Many older Omaha homes, from Dundee and Benson to Florence and the older parts of South Omaha, have brick chimneys. The metal flashing and mortar around them wear out and are a classic leak point.
  • Plumbing vent boots. The rubber collar around each vent pipe dries out and cracks after years of summer sun and winter cold. A cracked boot can drip straight onto a ceiling below.
  • Valleys. Where two roof slopes meet, water runs heavy and fast. Worn or badly cut valleys leak.
  • Skylights and roof vents. Seals and flashing around them fail over time, and hail can crack the covers.
  • Wind-lifted shingles. Strong storm winds can lift and crease shingles so they no longer seal down, letting wind-driven rain under them.
  • Ice dams at the eaves. In winter, meltwater can back up behind a ridge of ice at the roof edge and push under the shingles.

Why the stain is rarely right under the hole

Water that gets past the shingles often runs down the roof boards or along a rafter before it drips. A stain in the middle of a bedroom ceiling can come from a vent boot or flashing several feet higher up the roof. That is why patching the spot directly above a stain often does not fix the leak, and why finding the real entry point is most of the job.

How a roofer finds the leak

  1. Attic check. With a flashlight, the roofer follows water stains on the underside of the roof boards uphill to where they start.
  2. Roof inspection. They look closely at flashing, vent boots, valleys and any lifted, cracked or missing shingles near that spot.
  3. Water test. If it is still unclear, one person runs a hose on a small section of roof, starting low and working up, while another watches from the attic.

Winter leaks and ice dams

Omaha winters bring repeated freeze and thaw. When heat escaping from the house warms the upper roof, snow melts, runs down and refreezes at the cold eaves. The ridge of ice traps the next round of meltwater, which can back up under the shingles and drip into walls and ceilings. The lasting fix is usually in the attic, with better insulation and ventilation so the roof deck stays cold, plus a waterproof underlayment at the eaves when the roof is replaced. In the meantime, do not chip at ice with a hammer or axe, which damages shingles. Pulling snow off the lower edge with a long-handled roof rake from the ground is safer.

What you can do before the roofer arrives

  • Catch drips and move belongings out of the way.
  • Take photos of the stain and, if you can get into the attic safely, of any wet wood or insulation.
  • Note when it leaks: every rain, only in wind-driven rain from one direction, or only during snowmelt. That clue helps the roofer find the source faster.

Permits for roof work in Omaha

Small repairs and full replacements are treated differently, and the rules can change, so ask the contractor whether your job needs a permit and who will apply for it. For work that needs one, the City of Omaha says you must have an approved permit before construction starts, and its How to Get a Permit page includes a guide to scheduling a roof inspection with photos. Bellevue, Papillion, La Vista and the other suburbs run their own building departments, so check with your city if you live outside Omaha.
