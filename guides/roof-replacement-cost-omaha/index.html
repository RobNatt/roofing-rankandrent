Roof Replacement Cost in Omaha

Published roof prices for Omaha range from under $6,000 to over $30,000, and both ends can be right. This guide shows where the numbers come from, what drives the spread, and how to read the quotes you get.

The short answer

Published figures for replacing a roof in the Omaha area range from about $6,000 to more than $30,000. Both ends can be accurate, because they describe very different jobs:

Where the Omaha figures come from

Cost vs Value 2025 (Omaha)

The 2025 Cost vs Value report for Omaha, published on JLC Online, lists an asphalt shingle roof replacement at a job cost of $27,900 in Omaha, compared with $30,693 for the West North Central region and $31,871 as the national average. The metal version of the project is $46,778 in Omaha.

That is a big, fully specified job. The report's 2025 project description calls for removing the old roof down to the wood sheathing, then installing 30 squares (3,000 sq ft) of laminated shingles with new underlayment, galvanized drip edge and aluminum flashing, on a hip roof with custom flashing at two skylights and a vented ridge. In other words, it is priced for a large roof with some complicated details, done to a high standard.

This Old House 2026 (Nebraska)

The This Old House Nebraska cost page (updated June 2026) puts the statewide average roof replacement at $7,473, with a range of $5,825 to $19,272 depending on material. It lists asphalt at $4.55 per square foot, metal at $8.35, wood at $10.80, clay at $14.98 and slate at $15.05. The page says it based its estimates on RSMeans material cost data and a typical-size Nebraska home with a medium-pitch roof. That is a much smaller and simpler roof than the Cost vs Value project, and it is a statewide figure, not Omaha only.

Why the numbers differ so much

Put the two sources side by side and the per-square-foot gap explains most of the difference.

EXAMPLE Our own arithmetic using the published figures above. Cost vs Value 2025: $27,900 ÷ 3,000 sq ft = about $9.30 per sq ft of roof. This Old House 2026: $4.55 per sq ft for asphalt. For a 2,000 sq ft roof, that gives 2,000 × $4.55 = $9,100 at the lower rate and 2,000 × $9.30 = $18,600 at the higher rate. Neither is a quote. They show how much the scope and assumptions change the answer.

The things that push a quote toward the high end are:

  • Roof size, not house size. Roofers price by the "square", which is 100 sq ft of roof surface. Overhangs and pitch make the roof bigger than the floor plan. A two-story house with the same floor space has a smaller roof than a ranch, but it is higher and harder to work on.
  • Pitch and height. Steep roofs, common on older homes in midtown neighborhoods, need extra safety gear and go slower.
  • Tear-off. Removing one or more old layers and hauling them away adds labor and dump fees.
  • Decking. Soft or rotted roof boards found during tear-off must be replaced. Good contracts state a per-sheet price for this up front.
  • Details. Chimneys, skylights, valleys, dormers and hips all need extra flashing and cutting.
  • Underlayment and ice protection. Because of Omaha's freeze-thaw winters, many roofers add a waterproof underlayment along the eaves to resist ice dams. Ask whether your quote includes it and how far up the roof it goes.
  • Ventilation. Adding or correcting ridge and soffit vents costs more now but helps the new roof last and reduces ice dams.
  • Materials. Basic three-tab shingles, laminated (architectural) shingles, impact-resistant shingles and metal all price differently.
  • Timing. After a big hail storm, demand for roofers spikes across the metro, which can affect prices and wait times.

Asphalt, impact-resistant or metal?

Most Omaha homes have asphalt shingles, and they are the lowest-cost option in both sources above. Laminated shingles are thicker than older three-tab shingles and are the default for most re-roofs today. Impact-resistant shingles are built to take hail better. The Nebraska Department of Insurance (accessed 2026) notes that rebuilding with impact-resistant roofing may qualify you for a homeowners insurance discount, so ask your agent what the discount would be and weigh it against the extra cost. Metal costs considerably more up front, about 1.7 times the asphalt job in the 2025 Omaha Cost vs Value figures ($46,778 against $27,900).

Will a new roof pay for itself when you sell?

Usually not in full. The 2025 Cost vs Value report for Omaha estimates that an asphalt roof replacement adds $7,476 in resale value, recouping 26.8% of its $27,900 cost. The same report's national average is 67.5%, so in this data a new roof added less resale value in Omaha than it did nationally. A sound roof still matters when selling, because buyers and inspectors notice an old one, but replace a roof because it needs it, not as an investment.

What insurance does and does not pay

If hail or wind damaged the roof, your homeowners policy may pay for some or all of the replacement, minus your deductible. Insurance does not pay to replace a roof that is simply old. Many policies pay the actual cash value first and the rest after the work is done, within a deadline that the Department of Insurance says is usually 180 days. How to compare quotes

Get at least two or three written quotes and line them up item by item. Make sure each one says:

  • The roof area in squares, and how it was measured.
  • Whether all old layers will be torn off.
  • The shingle brand, product line and color.
  • Underlayment type, and where ice and water protection goes.
  • New flashing, drip edge, pipe boots and vents, or which old parts are reused.
  • The price per sheet for any decking that needs replacing.
  • Cleanup, magnetic nail sweep and disposal.
  • Who pulls the permit. The City of Omaha says permitted work needs an approved permit before construction starts (City of Omaha Planning Department).
  • The workmanship warranty, in writing, and what the manufacturer's warranty requires.
  • The payment schedule. Be wary of large up-front deposits.

A much lower price usually means something is missing, such as a full tear-off, new flashing or ice protection. A much higher price may include upgrades you did not ask for. Ask each contractor to explain the difference.

Before you sign

  • Check the contractor's state registration in the Nebraska Department of Labor search.
  • Ask for proof of liability insurance and workers' compensation.
  • If insurance is paying, remember that Nebraska bans contractors from paying or rebating your deductible (Neb. Rev. Stat. 44-8604) and gives you a right to cancel within three business days (Neb. Rev. Stat. 44-8603).

Costs people forget to budget for

The roof quote is not always the whole bill. Ask about these items before work starts so they do not show up as surprises halfway through the job:

  • Extra decking. Nobody can see the boards until the old shingles come off. Get the per-sheet price in writing and ask to see photos of any boards that are replaced.
  • Gutters. Old gutters can be bent during tear-off, and hail often dents them. Decide up front whether they are being replaced, reset or left alone.
  • Skylights and chimneys. An old skylight is often replaced with the roof, because re-flashing a worn unit can lead to leaks later. Chimney flashing and crowns may also need work.
  • Attic ventilation. Adding vents or baffles costs a little now, but poor ventilation shortens shingle life and feeds ice dams.
  • Permit and inspection. Check whether the permit fee is in the price, and who schedules the inspection.
  • Interior repairs. Ceiling and drywall damage from an old leak is usually a separate job for a different trade.

If your insurer is paying for storm damage, ask the adjuster which of these items are in their estimate. Items missed in the first estimate can sometimes be added later as a supplement, with photos and an explanation from your roofer.

Is it repair or replacement?

Before budgeting for a whole roof, make sure you need one. Many leaks and storm problems are local repairs that cost a small fraction of a replacement. Read roof repair or replacement? how to decide for a simple checklist.
