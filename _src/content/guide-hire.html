The short version

  1. Check the roofer is registered in the Nebraska Department of Labor contractor search.
  2. Ask for their insurance certificate and confirm it with the insurer.
  3. Get two or three written, itemized estimates for the same scope of work.
  4. Read the whole contract before you sign, including the warranty and payment schedule.
  5. Walk away from anyone who offers to cover your insurance deductible. In Nebraska that is against the law.

1. Check that the roofer is registered in Nebraska

Nebraska's Contractor Registration Act says a contractor must be registered with the Department of Labor before doing construction work in the state, including repairs (Neb. Rev. Stat. 48-2104). The only exceptions are people working on their own property and anyone earning under $5,000 a year from construction. A real roofing company should be registered under the same business name that appears on its estimate.

Look the company up in the Nebraska Department of Labor contractor registration search. If you cannot find it, ask why before you go any further. Registration is a state requirement. Cities can add their own rules, so for permitted work, ask the City of Omaha or your suburb's building department too.

2. Confirm their insurance

Ask for a certificate of insurance showing general liability coverage, and workers' compensation if they have employees. Then call the insurance company on the certificate to confirm the policy is active. Roofing is dangerous work, and you do not want a fall or a damaged neighbor's car to become your problem.

3. Get two or three written estimates

For anything beyond a small patch, get at least two written estimates. Compare the scope line by line, not just the total. One estimate may include tearing off the old roof, new underlayment and replacing soft decking, while a cheaper one quietly leaves those out. 4. Questions to ask every roofer

  • Are you registered with the Nebraska Department of Labor, and under what name?
  • Can I see your insurance certificate?
  • How long have you worked in the Omaha area, and can I see recent jobs nearby?
  • Who will actually do the work: your own crew or a subcontractor?
  • What exactly is included, and what costs extra if you find rotten decking?
  • Does this job need a permit, and who pulls it?
  • What does your workmanship warranty cover, and for how long? Is it in writing?
  • What payment do you need, and when?
  • How will you protect my landscaping, and how do you clean up nails?

5. What the contract should include

  • The full scope: tear-off or overlay, underlayment, flashing, vents, and the shingle brand and product line.
  • The price for replacing damaged decking, per sheet, if they find it once the old roof is off.
  • Start and finish dates, and what happens if weather delays the job.
  • Cleanup, a magnetic nail sweep and disposal.
  • Who pulls the permit. For permitted work, the City of Omaha says you need an approved permit before construction starts (City of Omaha Planning Department).
  • The workmanship warranty in writing, and what you must do to keep the manufacturer's warranty valid.
  • The payment schedule.

6. If insurance is paying: your rights under Nebraska law

Nebraska's Insured Homeowners Protection Act covers roofing contracts paid from a homeowners insurance claim. Under it:

  • You can cancel. You can cancel the contract in writing until midnight on the later of the third business day after you sign, or the third business day after your insurer tells you in writing that all or part of the claim is not covered (Neb. Rev. Stat. 44-8603).
  • No deductible deals. A contractor may not promise to rebate any part of your deductible, including through a discount or an allowance off the price (44-8604). The contract, estimate or work order must carry a notice saying so, and the law makes you, the homeowner, responsible for paying the deductible (44-8607).
  • An itemized breakdown first. Before work starts, the contractor must give you and your insurer an itemized description of the work, materials, labor and fees, and the total (44-8606).
  • Read any assignment carefully. If a roofer asks you to assign your insurance benefits to them, Nebraska law sets rules for that document, including a printed warning that you are handing over rights under your policy (44-8605). Do not sign one you do not understand.

7. Red flags

  • Door-knocking right after a storm, especially from a company with no Omaha address or local phone number.
  • Pressure to sign today, or a "storm special" that expires tonight.
  • An offer to cover or "work with" your deductible. In Nebraska that is illegal (44-8604).
  • An assignment of benefits pushed on you before you have even talked to your insurer.
  • A large cash deposit up front, or a request to pay in full before work starts.
  • No written estimate, a vague one-line scope, or no written warranty.
  • Not in the state registration search, or registered under a different name than the one on the estimate.

8. Check reviews and references

Read Google reviews for the company's own business profile, and look at the recent ones, not just the star average. Ask for two or three addresses of jobs they finished in your part of town in the last year. A roofer with real local work is usually happy to share them.
