How it works

Omaha Roofing Experts is a referral service, not a roofing company. We do not send out our own crews. Our job is to get your request in front of an independent roofing contractor who works in your part of the metro, so you are not stuck calling down a list.

  1. Call or send the form. Tell us your address or ZIP code, what you are seeing (a leak, missing shingles, hail dents) and how urgent it is.

  3. The contractor contacts you. They arrange the inspection, give you their own estimate and answer your questions. You decide whether to hire them. You are never obliged to.

Any price, schedule or warranty comes from the contractor you hire, not from this website. Before you sign anything, check the points in our storm contractor checklist.

Why Omaha roofs take a beating

Omaha gets the full range of Plains weather, and a roof here has to handle all of it.

  • Hail and wind. Spring and summer thunderstorms can bring hail and strong straight-line winds. Hail can bruise shingles in ways you cannot see from the ground, and wind can lift and crease shingles so they stop sealing. See storm and hail damage.
  • Freeze and thaw. Winter temperatures swing above and below freezing. Water that gets into small gaps freezes, expands and widens them, and snow that melts and refreezes at the eaves can build ice dams that push water under shingles. See roof leak repair.
  • Summer heat and sun. Heat and UV slowly dry out shingles and the rubber boots around plumbing vents, which is one of the most common leak points on any roof.

That mix is why a roof that looked fine last fall can start leaking after one hard spring storm, and why it pays to have a roofer check things after any storm with hail you could hear.

Before you hire any roofer

  • Check that the contractor is registered with the state using the Nebraska Department of Labor contractor search.
  • Ask for proof of liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage.
  • Get a written, itemized estimate. If insurance is paying, Nebraska law requires the contractor to give you and your insurer an itemized breakdown before work starts (Neb. Rev. Stat. 44-8606).
  • Ask whether the job needs a permit and who will pull it. For work that needs one, the City of Omaha says you must have an approved permit before construction starts (City of Omaha Planning Department).
